# LinkedIn AEO/GEO Optimization Methodology

Use this reference when planning an optimization experiment, predicting URL concepts, or interpreting post-publication observations.

## Evidence stance

The method separates established inputs from hypotheses and observations:

- **Known:** the exact post copy, opening, hashtags, generated LinkedIn URL, test conditions, and directly observed retrieval outputs.
- **Inferred:** which concepts are most salient, which positioning association a reader may form, and which terms may appear in a generated URL.
- **Hypothesized:** causal relationships among opening text, hashtags, URL semantics, and AI retrieval or interpretation.

Do not convert a plausible mechanism, isolated observation, or correlation into a platform claim.

## Hypotheses under test

- **H1:** Opening text may influence LinkedIn's generated URL.
- **H2:** Hashtags may influence LinkedIn's generated URL.
- **H3:** Generated URL semantics may influence how AI systems retrieve or interpret the post.

LinkedIn's implementation and external retrieval systems can change. Treat every result as time- and context-bound.

## Pre-publication method

1. Identify the post's subject, point of view, evidence, and intended audience.
2. Select the most relevant positioning territory for this post. The profile's primary territory is only one candidate.
3. Record one intended target association and a small set of supporting concepts.
4. Draft an opening that states the subject and substantive point in natural language.
5. Add only hashtags that improve categorization or reader navigation.
6. Before publishing, record the exact opening, hashtags, and predicted URL concepts in the private experiment log.
7. Perform a credibility pass. If an optimization weakens accuracy, nuance, or readability, remove it.

Predicted URL concepts are semantic terms, not an exact promised slug. Favor concepts that are prominent, specific, and central to the post; do not reverse-engineer awkward copy around them.

## Post-publication method

1. Copy the actual LinkedIn URL exactly as generated.
2. Compare its semantic terms with the pre-registered prediction.
3. Record retrieval tests with the date, system or interface, exact query, relevant settings or sign-in state, and observed result.
4. Separate direct retrieval of the post from looser thematic association with the author.
5. Note plausible confounders such as account authority, engagement, indexing delay, query wording, personalization, model version, and web-access differences.
6. Classify the conclusion as **supports**, **does not support**, or **inconclusive** for each tested hypothesis.

One post cannot establish causation. Look for repeated patterns across posts and change one meaningful variable at a time when practical. Preserve contradictory and null results.

## Quality gate

A post is ready only when:

- A reader can identify the topic and point from the opening.
- The selected positioning target fits the actual substance.
- Claims match the available evidence and retain necessary qualifiers.
- Key concepts appear naturally and consistently.
- Hashtags are useful rather than decorative.
- URL and retrieval predictions are labeled as hypotheses.
- The post still sounds like the author.
